Triage core

📞 30-second callback triage
"I'm managing another time-sensitive neurosurgical patient. Tell me the current neurologic examination, what changed, when it changed, the critical imaging finding, anticoagulation status, and what you need from neurosurgery right now."

Ask in this order:

  • 1. Are airway, pupils, consciousness, blood pressure, or motor examination acutely abnormal?
  • 2. What changed that prompted the call now?
  • 3. Best, worst, and current examination?
  • 4. When was the patient last known to be at baseline?
  • 5. What imaging is available, and what is the dangerous finding?
  • 6. Anticoagulated, recently postoperative, or carrying a device?
  • 7. What action is being requested?
  • 8. Who is physically with the patient and repeating the examination?

Built on TeamSTEPPS principles — SBAR, call-outs, check-backs, closed-loop communication.[6] Assign only a provisional E# / O# / W at intake — do not run the full complexity score during the callback.

🚨 E-axis & E3 override triggers
LevelMeaning
E3 EmergentActive or imminent threat. Interrupt routine work, assess immediately, escalate now.
E2 UrgentMeaningful deterioration/window risk. Active evaluation & decision generally within 1–6 h, often sooner.
E1 Time-sensitiveComplete this shift or within ~6–24 h.
E0 RoutineCan await scheduled review / later work block.

Intracranial E3 triggers

  • Acute decline in consciousness · new anisocoria or nonreactive pupil · new posturing
  • Rapidly progressive focal deficit · acute hydrocephalus with deterioration
  • EVD obstruction with deterioration or uncontrolled ICP · severe mass effect / impending herniation
  • Posterior fossa lesion with brainstem compression or obstructive hydrocephalus
  • Uncontrolled seizure / status epilepticus · sudden postop decline · postop hematoma with deterioration
  • Uncontrolled intracranial hemorrhage · severe edema with a herniation syndrome
Do not wait for a complete Cushing triad — the full combination is uncommon and often late. Acute altered consciousness + ipsilateral pupillary dilation + contralateral weakness = classic transtentorial herniation.[1]

Spinal E3 triggers

  • Rapidly progressive weakness · new loss of ambulation
  • Acute bowel/bladder dysfunction with compatible findings · urinary retention with saddle symptoms or new weakness
  • Acute cord or cauda equina compression · epidural hematoma or abscess with neurologic change
  • Unstable injury with deficit · high cervical lesion with respiratory compromise
Confirmed cauda equina compression is a spinal emergency. A "48-hour interval" is not permission to wait — decompress as soon as practically possible, especially for incomplete or progressive syndromes.[2]

Vascular E3 triggers

  • Suspected aneurysmal rebleeding · unsecured ruptured aneurysm
  • Acute decline with a vascular lesion · time-window ischemia / LVO
  • Expanding pseudoaneurysm or active extravasation · BCVI with evolving ischemia · post-procedure ICH

Aneurysmal SAH: identify and treat the ruptured aneurysm promptly, preferably within 24 hours.[3]

Systemic / perioperative E3 triggers

  • Airway failure · severe hypoxemia · shock · active major hemorrhage
  • ICH requiring urgent antithrombotic reversal · severe coagulopathy when intervention may be needed
  • Sepsis/septic shock with intracranial hardware · exposed implant with infection or neurologic deterioration
  • CSF leak with wound failure and instability · competing emergency operation affecting neurologic safety
🔪 O-axis: operative trajectory
LevelMeaning
O3Operative now — emergency procedure underway or decision effectively made.
O2Likely operative this shift/day; limited decisions remain.
O1Potentially operative — depends on evolution, imaging, optimization, exam, or senior review.
O0Nonoperative currently — observation, medical, outpatient, or another service.

Re-score whenever: imaging changes · exam changes · repeat imaging is stable · senior review occurs · contraindications resolve · a procedure is completed · goals of care change.

O0 ≠ low acuity. An unsecured ruptured aneurysm, acute hydrocephalus, status epilepticus, or severe intracranial hypertension may be E3 while not immediately requiring an open operation.
🧩 C-axis: 11 domains & modifiers

Score each domain 0–3, highest applicable. Max 33. Thresholds (operational, unvalidated): 0–5 C1 low · 6–11 C2 moderate · 12–23 C3 high · 24–33 C4 extreme.

Domains: 1 Neuro instability/trajectory · 2 Lesion & anatomic danger · 3 Hidden-deterioration risk (W) · 4 Diagnostic uncertainty · 5 Baseline & exam interpretability · 6 Prior anatomy/devices/procedures · 7 Procedural/operative complexity · 8 Hemostasis & medication risk · 9 Infection/wound/CSF · 10 Medical/physiologic · 11 Communication/consent/dispo/task burden.

W3 mandatory interaction with E-axis: W3 should generally not remain E0. If W3 is E1 rather than E2, document why immediate evaluation is not required. Define the reassessment interval, responsible examiner, and escalation trigger.

Modifiers

Always-record: W hidden-deterioration risk · NΔ changing exam · AC anticoag/antiplatelet issue · VASC vascular lesion/window · OR active operative prep.

Optional: DEV device · INF infection/wound · BASE? baseline uncertain · ANAT altered anatomy · IMG critical imaging pending · CONSENT unresolved · DISPO bottleneck · XFER transfer · MULTI multiple services · OWN? ownership unclear.

Clinical reference boxes

🩸 Traumatic-lesion thresholds (BTF / ACS)

Reference thresholds, not substitutes for senior review.[4]

  • EDH: evacuate when volume > 30 cm³ regardless of GCS.
  • Nonoperative EDH requires all of: volume < 30 cm³, thickness < 15 mm, MLS < 5 mm, GCS > 8, no focal deficit — with serial CT and close observation in a neurosurgical center.
  • Acute SDH: evacuate for thickness > 10 mm or MLS > 5 mm regardless of GCS.
  • Comatose patient with a smaller SDH may still meet operative criteria for: GCS decline ≥ 2 points, asymmetric or fixed/dilated pupils, or ICP > 20 mm Hg.
  • Traumatic parenchymal lesions: progressive deterioration, refractory intracranial hypertension, or significant mass effect each independently support operative consideration.
ACS TBI 2024 (broader best-practice trigger, not identical to BTF lesion-specific thresholds): evacuate a large traumatic hematoma > 25 mL before neurologic deterioration; posterior fossa lesions may warrant a lower threshold.

ICP-threshold clarification

Older BTF SDH surgical criteria use ICP > 20 mm Hg as one operative trigger (comatose, smaller SDH). More recent general severe-TBI guidance uses 22 mm Hg as an initial treatment threshold (reasonable treatment range 20–25 mm Hg sustained > 5 min). Do not silently substitute one for the other — they come from different guideline contexts.[4]

Use institutional pharmacy and neurocritical-care protocols for final dosing.

Capture: exact drug · indication · last dose · renal function · INR/PTT/anti-Xa/fibrinogen/platelets · recent lytic · mechanical valve · recent DVT/PE · recent stent · reversal ordered → administered → lab response · restart deadline · who owns the restart decision.

🦠 Shunt/CSF infection (IDSA)

Confirmed infected CSF shunt: complete removal + temporary EVD + IV antimicrobials. Leaving infected hardware historically has low success; a randomized pediatric study reported 70% recurrence without removal. Even with removal + external drainage, failure/reinfection still occurs in ~10–20%.[5]

Reimplantation timing

  • CoNS or C. acnes, no CSF abnormalities: potentially as early as day 3 after removal if cultures negative ×48 h.
  • Same organisms with CSF abnormalities: generally after 7 days of therapy if repeat cultures negative.
  • S. aureus or gram-negative bacilli: generally 10 days after CSF cultures become negative.
  • A routine antibiotic-free observation period before reimplantation is not recommended.

Guideline reference points — final timing is organism-, patient-, and institution-dependent. Obtain cultures before antibiotics when possible without delaying unstable-patient treatment.

Workflow

⚓ Task-switching anchor

Before leaving any patient, record: current state | next physical action | deadline or trigger | owner. A physical action begins with a verb and can be completed without reinterpreting what you meant.

Avoid

"Follow up" · "Work on shunt" · "Finish consult" · "Check imaging" · "Figure out disposition"

Use

  • "Compare current CT with CT from last confirmed shunt failure."
  • "Call mother to establish baseline gaze, speech, and behavior."
  • "Measure maximal SDH thickness and midline shift."
  • "Confirm PCC administration and repeat INR."
  • "Review repeat CT at 22:00." · "Page chief with exam, measurements, and anticoagulation status."
🪜 Consult stages & pause points
  • 1. Intake (30–90 s): only enough to classify urgency. Pause 1: provisional E#/O#/W. No full complexity score yet.
  • 2. Preserve the interrupted task (<30 s): write the anchor, assign a deadline, name the bedside examiner, set an alarm. Pause 2: interrupted patient has a restart point.
  • 3. Focused chart reconstruction (5–10 min max): baseline → current change → NSG history → device/setting → prior failure phenotype → key op note → key prior imaging → antithrombotics → infection history → competing issues. Don't read chronologically; convert unanswered questions into specific later tasks. Pause 3: one-sentence problem representation.
  • 4. Bedside: safe now? objective change? exam reliable? syndrome matches imaging? what must happen before I leave? urgency increased? Pause 4: update E/O, place immediate safety orders.
  • 5. Imaging & data synthesis. Pause 5: senior-ready package — one-liner + best/worst/current exam + measurements + AC/labs + operative question + proposed next step.
  • 6. Senior decision & contingency: operative? timing? level of care? exam frequency? repeat imaging? BP/Na goals? med/device/antithrombotic plan? deterioration trigger? Pause 6: every task has action | owner | deadline | trigger.
  • 7. Destination bundle (see below). Pause 7: a destination is complete only when accepted and operational, not proposed.
  • 8. Closure & downgrade checkpoint (see Close flow on each card).

🏥 Admissions, booking, consents, discharges

Admission

  • Med rec: Nonsurgical tab → Admission → Outside Meds → dispense report → sticky note (#HTN-home amlodipine…) → copy into H&P.
  • Orders: continue/dc all current orders; start all home meds except ACE-i + blood thinners perioperatively (blood thinners held 14 d after TBI). Baclofen withdrawal can be lethal — don't miss home meds.
  • Preop: STAT PT/PTT/T&S ordered-collected-resulted; NPO MN or sips with meds.
  • DVT ppx: hold 24 h after fresh TBI, 6 h after fresh EVD/procedure, after midnight for next-day preop.
  • Order sets: "Neurosurgery Brain Admission" / "Neurosurgery Spine Admission". H&P: .ewhp + .ewbon/.rihnsbon; plans .rihnscranialplanA / .rihnsspineplanA.
  • We admit non-top TBIs (tSAH can go home), brain tumors, spine fractures (non-op compression fx can go home).

Booking

  • Orders → "case request". Location RIH Main vs HCH. Real procedure name in description; laterality exact; watch auto-change on Accept.
  • Urgent DHC = life-threatening emergency classification. Clarify name + classification with attending first — hard to change later.
  • ICU postop → select ICU post-op. Message Teams Booking chat (name, attending, date, in-house vs home). Call OR front desk (45647) if emergent/same-day/next-day.

Consent

  • Consentable = A+O×3 reliably → electronic in person; otherwise phone consent with witness.
  • List all plausible attendings. Cranial: AM PS CD DC KS CL CC · Vascular: CD RT KM · TSSH: CD DC CL PS + ENT.
  • Inside OR: copy-paste the exact booking name (spaces, typos and all); laterality must match booking.
  • Paper: no abbreviations, never cross out — reprint. Date + time next to every signature. Drop at OR front desk, refresh until scanned.
  • Interpreter: record RIH language line ID# + date/time. Leave the consent tab when done or nobody else can access it.

Discharge

  • Med rec → all meds continued/stopped → Sign & E-prescribe. Post-op: usually oxycodone (10 tabs, no refills) + Narcan + bowel regimen; dc IV meds.
  • Update follow-up providers (CoC) — every consulted service. Instructions: .nsdischarge. Summary: .ewdcsummary (+.ewdc for course); include last exam.
  • If meds change after signing the summary, refresh the summary. Sign AVS → discharge order pops → sign.

ℹ What counts as each E level / E3 triggers
E3 — active or imminent neurologic/physiologic threat: interrupt, assess immediately, escalate now. · E2 — meaningful risk of deterioration or loss of treatment window: evaluate and decide within 1–6 h, often sooner. · E1 — complete this shift / ~6–24 h. · E0 — can safely await scheduled review. Windows are operational approximations, not disease definitions.

ℹ What counts as each O level / when to re-score
O3 — emergency procedure underway or decision effectively made. · O2 — high probability of operation this shift/day; limited decisions remain. · O1 — intervention plausible but depends on evolution, imaging, optimization, exam, or senior review. · O0 — observation, medical treatment, outpatient, or another service presently favored.
Re-score whenever: imaging changes · exam changes · repeat imaging is stable · senior review occurs · contraindications resolve · a procedure is completed · goals of care change. O0 ≠ low acuity. Unsecured ruptured aneurysm, acute hydrocephalus, status epilepticus, or severe intracranial hypertension can be E3 while O0.
ℹ What counts as each W level / W3 lesions
W0 — low likelihood of rapid or clinically silent progression. · W1 — routine serial exam or imaging required. · W2 — known evolving disease: explicit reassessment time + contingency required. · W3 — reassuring present exam but substantial lesion-specific risk of abrupt deterioration.
Classic W3 lesions: EDH with GCS 15 · large SDH with preserved exam · expanding contusion · posterior fossa hemorrhage · acute hydrocephalus · anticoagulated ICH · shunt malfunction with subtle presentation · unsecured ruptured aneurysm · spinal epidural process with subtle weakness · new postop headache/vomiting/somnolence/wound swelling · incomplete imaging despite a dangerous syndrome. W3 should generally not remain E0. If W3 stays E1 (not E2), document why immediate evaluation isn't required — and define the reassessment interval, responsible examiner, and escalation trigger.
W3 with E≤1: W3 should generally not remain E0/E1. Either raise E, or document below why immediate evaluation is not required — and define the reassessment interval, examiner, and escalation trigger.
